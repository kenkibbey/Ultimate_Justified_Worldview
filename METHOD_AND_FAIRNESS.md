# Method and fairness — provisional, open to challenge

We examine **exact claims**, their strongest charitable interpretation, and relevant alternatives—not simplified labels for whole religions or philosophies. Worldview families may contain very different versions.

## Basic distinctions

- **Truth:** whether a statement really corresponds to reality.
- **Justification:** whether our reasons and evidence warrant accepting it.
- **Knowledge:** a disputed category we must investigate, not an assumed equivalent of certainty.
- **Confidence:** how strongly the overall case presently warrants the proposition within a stated scope.
- **Completeness:** how much of the reality puzzle our findings cover.

A belief can be justified yet mistaken. A true claim might not be known to be true. We should not confuse confidence with proof.

## Nine open tests

We ask about (1) coherence, (2) correspondence with relevant evidence, (3) explanatory scope, (4) explanatory depth, (5) ability to distinguish rivals, (6) warranted premises/inferences, (7) parsimony, (8) resilience to serious objections, and (9) calibrated uncertainty and readiness to revise. This is a checklist for reasoning, not a fixed numerical score.

## Two levels

First, what warrant does an individual claim have? Second, does the connected set of supported claims better account for reality without papering over conflict? A convincing story is not enough. If an essential proposition fails under appropriate rules, good storytelling cannot offset its failure. Rejection of one specific version is not rejection of every related worldview.

## No invisible starting assumptions

We provisionally use observation, reason, logic, memory, testimony and methods of checking for errors. Their reliability remains examinable. Radical skepticism, infinite regress, circularity, unjustified stopping points, the problem of the criterion, and alternative logical assumptions may all challenge our method.

## What can change our minds?

Specific source corrections, stronger arguments, new evidence, misinterpretation, failed assumptions, or better alternatives. We preserve prior reasoning and any impacts on dependent claims. We do not treat a lack of proof as proof of falsehood.

No major tradition starts as a selected winner. Skeptical suspension is permitted but not mandated indefinitely. We respect community context and restrictions on sensitive or Indigenous knowledge. Public synthesis is limited to findings actually examined. Internal AI objection generation is not independent human review.
